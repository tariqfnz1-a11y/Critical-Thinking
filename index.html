🔍 Questioning

Challenge 1 — Can You Trust the Claim?

A student says:

"Every time I sleep less than 6 hours, I get a lower maths mark. Therefore, sleeping less than 6 hours always causes low maths marks."
What is the best critical‑thinking response?
Think: does one thing happening after another prove it caused it? What else could affect maths marks?
❌ Finding Errors

Challenge 2 — Find the Mistake

3 × 4 = 12
3 × 5 = 15
3 × 6 = 18
∴ 3 × 100 = 300

A student says the pattern proves the answer.
Is the student's reasoning correct?
Multiplication by 3 follows a clear rule—does it work for every number?
🔢 Testing Patterns

Challenge 3 — Does the Pattern Always Work?

A student notices:
1 + 3 = 4   |   2 + 4 = 6   |   3 + 5 = 8
They conclude: "Adding two odd numbers always gives an even number."
What should a critical thinker do next?
Try 5 + 7, or 11 + 13. What do you notice? Is there any case where it fails?
📊 Evidence

Challenge 4 — Which Evidence Is Stronger?

A school wants to know whether students learn mathematics better when they practise for 20 minutes every day.
Which evidence would be most useful?
Which option involves data and a fair comparison?
🧩 Assumptions

Challenge 5 — What's Missing?

Ahmed says:

"I scored higher after changing schools, so the new school must have caused my improvement."
What should Ahmed consider?
Two events happening together doesn't always mean one caused the other. What else changed?
⚖️ Multiple Solutions

Challenge 6 — Which Solution Is Better?

A rectangle has an area of 36 cm².
6 cm × 6 cm
Area = 36 cm²
4 cm × 9 cm
Area = 36 cm²
What can we conclude?
Compare the two rectangles—do they have the same shape but the same area?
📈 Misleading Information

Challenge 7 — Is the Graph Misleading?

A graph shows:
Class A: 80%    Class B: 85%

But the graph's vertical axis begins at 75% rather than 0%.
Why might this be misleading?
Think about how the visual height difference compares to the actual percentage gap.
💡 Counterexamples

Challenge 8 — Can You Disprove It?

A student claims:

"All rectangles have four equal sides."
What is the best way to challenge the claim?
A single counterexample can disprove a statement that claims "all". What shape is a rectangle but not a square?
🔄 Changing Your Mind

Challenge 9 — What Would Change Your Mind?

You believe:

"Students should have maths homework every day because more practice always produces better results."
Which evidence would be most likely to make you reconsider?
What kind of evidence would be strong enough to challenge a deeply held belief?
🧠 Reflection

Challenge 10 — Become the Investigator

Claim: "Students who use calculators are worse at mathematics."

You must decide whether the claim is reasonable.
What evidence would you want before accepting or rejecting the claim?
